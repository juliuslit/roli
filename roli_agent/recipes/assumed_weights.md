Column `amount_basis` (added 2026-11-03, Phase 2D): `per_plate` for every restaurant-usage row (the range is what one plate carries; in a recipe for N servings it enters the calculator as a per-serving amount, so the whole recipe gets N times it), `per_count_unit` for rows that describe one item (W25 egg yolk, W28b little gem head), multiplied by the line's stated count. Phase 2C had entered per-plate amounts as whole-recipe amounts, which understated them per recipe serving by the serving count; Checkpoint 2D reports the correction. The three `proposed_from_evidence` rows use the evidence gathered in Checkpoint 2C.1 section 5 and are usable in experimental mode only; the blocked rows W03b, W10, W20 and W28 are unchanged and still never used.

Rows W30-W49 (added 2026-11-04, Phase 3 local-testing milestone, Julius's decision to allow experimental ingredient-category defaults): bounded per-plate amounts for common cheese garnishes and components, cured meats and dressings named on a menu but absent from the recipe, each on one named USDA record (a composition proxy where the exact food has no record, flagged as such), usable in experimental mode only, `proposed_unreviewed`, never approved. Column `dish_types` (new) restricts a row to the listed portion dish types (empty = any): W31 serves every dish type except pizza, W32 only pizza, because a named cheese on a pizza weighs several times a cheese garnish. Deep-frying oil, butter sauces and other materially uncertain ingredients have no default and stay open on purpose.

Rows W42-W49 (2026-11-04, after the seafood chalkboard and Flour + Water reruns): vegetable and fruit sides named on the menu (watermelon, eggplant, broccoli rabe, turnip, mustard greens, stone fruit as peach, sweet peppers, fennel) on direct USDA records with trace saturated fat; they close a dish's range without moving it. Column `exclude_pattern` (new): a pattern on the line's own words that rules a row out (W15 black pepper never matches vegetable 'peppers'). Chanterelles stay open: the USDA record reports no saturated fat.

Column `substitutes_family` (2026-11-04): USDA description heads the row's food can stand in for. When a menu-only word hits such a row and the recipe already calculates a line of that family (a pizza recipe with mozzarella, the menu says caciocavallo), the low bound is 0 g (the menu names the kitchen's cheese, not an extra one) and the high bound is the row's amount (an addition at most). Cheese rows W05, W06, W30-W36; cured-meat rows W26, W37, W38.

Column `role_sensitive` (2026-11-05, Julius): for cheese, cured-meat, nut, seed and olive rows the ingredient's role on the plate decides which half of the row's range applies when the role is clear (a garnish word beside it on the menu or in the recipe line; named in a salad's or pizza's own name = substantial; listed beside a fish or meat main = garnish). Ambiguous roles keep the whole range. Rows: W05, W07, W22, W24, W26, W30-W38, W41. The assumption's basis records the role and the reason.

Rows W50-W55 (2026-11-05, after the Survey fried-dish estimates were blocked by side words on the Greyhound menu): lime wedge, pickles, chilli sauces, flavoured butters, peas and mushy peas. W53 (flavoured butter, 10-30 g, major) is the first butter row; emulsified sauces stay uncovered. W55 is the first row on a USDA Survey record.
Rows W56-W57 (2026-11-06, SPEC_3_mvp.md 12.9.10): W56 replaces the mayonnaise proxy for tartare sauce, so W40 no longer lists it. W57 is the first row whose amount scales with a key ingredient: 0.3-10.8 g of oil per 100 g of olives, worked out from label fat. Sources for both are in `assumed_weights.csv`. The W56 high-bound figures are for 28 g; the W57 figures are per 100 g of olives at the high end.
Rows W58-W59 (2026-11-06, SPEC_3_mvp.md 12.10.6): new potatoes, two to three per plate at USDA's 60 g each. W59 applies only when the menu says buttered; its USDA record carries the butter. Sources in `assumed_weights.csv`.
